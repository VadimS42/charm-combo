## About the game

Duskhaven is a town that wakes up at dusk, where succubi, mermaids, dryads and
slimes live next door to everyone else. You're new here, doing whatever work
pays, and you manage to meet four women in the worst way possible. Every
chapter is a match-3 puzzle; every puzzle cleared moves her a step from "get
out" to "stay".

**Who you'll meet**

- **Amara Duskrose**, a succubus you summoned by accident with a chalk circle
  you copied off a loose page in the library. She's more insulted by the
  sloppy chalk than by being summoned, and she's looking for the book that
  binds her, because burning it would set her free. "You summoned me with
  that pathetic chalk circle?"
- **Marina Waverly**, a mermaid you dragged out of her own cove, in front of
  her entire family, while she was secretly searching for something she
  can't admit she lost. She'll pay you to help. Strictly business. "I was
  floating. On my back."
- **Opal**, a slime who'd been hiding in a jar marked PICKLED NEWTS in the
  woods for thirty-one years. You opened it, and kicked her whole collection
  into the stream. "I was fine. It was quiet in there."
- **Hazel Mossgrove**, a dryad whose tree you were hired to cut down. You'd
  have struck just below her heart. "Don't even think about it."

**Features**

- 16 puzzle chapters, four for each of them, each with its own goal and move
  limit.
- Rockets, Starbursts and Mega Blasts, cursed shards to clear, and a Charm
  ring that fills up for a Charm Burst.
- Hammer, Shuffle and +3 Moves when a board gets tough, and an Item Shop for a
  head start. Running out of moves still pays a few stars, so you can grind.
- Choices in conversation lean each story warm or teasing, with 16 endings to
  find and a Gallery to keep them in.
- A gift for each of them, and 20 achievements.
- Full controller support. (Steam page: add "Steam Deck ready".)
- (itch.io page: add "Play in your browser, or download for Windows, Mac and
  Linux.")

## Content questions (18+)

What's actually in the game, so you can answer both stores accurately:

- Sexual content. Each story ends with a night scene with her, played over a
  full-screen picture (round 22), then a morning-after epilogue. As written,
  the scene's text is intimate but not graphic; it has a marked place for your
  own lines. **If you add explicit lines or explicit CGs, your answers to both
  stores change** (see below).
- Suggestive character art (revealing outfits; check your final CGs and
  answer for those too, since the survey covers what's in the build).
- All characters are adults.
- No violence, no strong language, no gambling, no real-money purchases (the
  Item Shop uses stars earned in the game).
- The game shows a one-time 18+ notice on first launch.

**Steam:** fill in the Mature Content part of the content survey
(Steamworks > your app > Edit Store Page > Content Survey) and describe it in
plain words. Tick the categories whose descriptions match what's in the build,
including the final CGs. Valve reviews the answers, and they decide who sees
the page by default; Steam's own age gate on the store page comes from them.

- Without explicit scenes or art, the "Some Nudity or Sexual Content" category
  is the likely fit, with a description such as: "Romance with sexual content
  between adults. Intimate night scenes, nothing graphic. All characters are
  adults."
- With explicit scenes or art, it's "Adult Only Sexual Content". Those games
  are hidden from players who haven't chosen to see that content, and since
  July 2025 Steam's rules also forbid content that breaks the rules of its
  payment processors and card networks ("certain kinds of adult only
  content"). Valve delisted many explicit games then, mostly ones with extreme
  themes; consensual sex between adults isn't named as banned, but the rule is
  vague, so read the current wording before you submit.

**itch.io:** on the game's Edit page, mark the project as adult content
(18+). In July 2025 itch.io hid all adult NSFW pages from browse and search
under pressure from payment processors. In August it put free adult pages back
(they need "No payments" set under Pricing) but not paid ones, while it worked
on payments, and it announced a content-warnings form that NSFW pages must fill
in to stay listed. A paid game with explicit scenes is the case most affected,
so check itch.io's current rules before you pick a price.

(Not legal advice: store rules change, so read the current wording in
Steamworks and on itch.io when you submit.)
